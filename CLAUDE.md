- **Trading:** max 200 trades; stocks priced ≥ $5 only; ETFs OK; Treasury bonds
  from US/UK/DE/FR/IT/NL only. No margin, no shorting, no crypto, no derivatives,
  no stock-secured debt. $25 commission per stock trade, $10 per bond trade.
- **IPS is 50 words (pitch) + 500 words (statement)**, Times New Roman 12pt,
  double-spaced, 1" margins, 3 pages max including title page, PDF ≤ 5 MB.
  **No graphics, charts, images, footnotes or citations permitted in the IPS.**
- **Trading Notes Analysis:** exactly 3 notes, each reproduced verbatim from
  WInS, each with a reflection of **100 words or fewer**.
- After Nov 6 the strategy is frozen. We may not redesign it in hindsight.

## Style
- Plain language beats jargon. A 2025 finale judge explicitly told teams to
  "fight against" complex terminology; the best solutions are straightforward.
- Authentic team voice. The rubric has a whole criterion for it.
- Never write a number we cannot trace to a script or a cited source.

## ⚠️ Explain things so beginners actually understand them

**We are high school students, not finance professionals.** Assume no prior
knowledge. If we do not understand what you told us, it is not useful to us, and
we cannot defend it to a judge. Every explanation must leave us able to repeat
the idea to someone else.

**Rules for any agent working in this repo:**

1. **Define every term the first time it appears.** "Duration", "defeasance",
   "annuity-due", "basis risk", "sequence-of-returns risk", "immunisation" —
   none of these can be used bare. One short sentence of plain English, then
   carry on. Do not send us to a glossary mid-explanation.
2. **Show the arithmetic.** Never just state a result. Write out the calculation
   with the actual numbers in it, so we can follow each step and redo it
   ourselves. "$363,440 × 0.045 = $16,355" teaches; "the balance accretes at the
   discount rate" does not.
3. **Say what something *is* before you say what it does.** A STRIP is a bond
   that pays nothing until it matures and then pays one fixed amount — *then*
   explain why that is useful here.
4. **Prefer the short word.** Buy, not acquire. Pay out, not disburse. Match,
   not immunise. Lock in, not hedge. If a technical term is genuinely the
   clearest word, use it — and define it.
5. **Use one concrete example instead of the general rule.** Walk through a
   single $50,000 payment before describing all ten.
6. **Flag what kind of thing a number is.** Is it a fact, a market price, a
   convention we chose, or an assumption? We keep confusing these, and judges
   will ask.
7. **No unexplained acronyms.** Not LDI, IPS, PV, TIPS, YTM, ETF — not on first
   use.
8. **If we ask a question that contains a mistake, correct the mistake first**,
   plainly and without softening it, then answer. We would rather be corrected
   now than at the finale.
9. **End hard explanations with a one-sentence version** we could say out loud
   to a friend.
10. **When we get something wrong, tell us.** Do not simplify by leaving out the
    part that makes something true. Simple is the goal; vague is not.

**The test:** could one of us re-explain this, unprompted, a week later, to
someone who has never heard of the competition? If not, it was written for the
wrong audience — rewrite it.

## Do not
- Draft finished deliverable prose that is passed off as the students' own work.
  Produce structure, analysis, numbers, options and critique; the students write
  the submitted text in their own voice. This is a competition rule, not a
  preference.
