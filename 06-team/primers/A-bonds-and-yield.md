# Primer A — What a bond is, and what "yield" means

*For P4. Read this, then explain it to the team in 3 minutes, without notes.
Numbers marked "example" are invented to keep the arithmetic simple. They are

## What a bond is

A bond is a **loan that you can buy and sell**. You lend money to someone — here,
the US government — and they promise two things in writing:

1. **Pay you back a fixed amount on a fixed date.** That amount is the **face
   value** (usually $1,000 per bond). The date is the **maturity date**.
2. **Pay you interest along the way.** That interest is the **coupon**, a fixed
   percentage of the face value, paid every six months for US government bonds.

The US government's bonds are called **Treasuries**. They are treated as the
safest dollar investment in the world, because the US government can always raise
taxes or create dollars to pay back a dollar debt.

### One example
In WInS there is a bond called **T-BOND 4.750% 15-Feb-2037**. The name tells you
everything:
- It is a US Treasury bond.
- It pays a **4.75% coupon**: 4.75% × $1,000 = **$47.50 a year**, paid as $23.75
  every six months.
- On **15 February 2037** the government pays back the **$1,000**.

## What "yield" means

You rarely buy a bond for exactly $1,000. Bonds trade every day, and their
**price** moves up and down. The **yield** (full name: *yield to maturity*) is the
yearly return you lock in **if you buy at today's price and hold the bond until
it matures**. It counts both the coupons *and* any gain or loss between the price
you paid and the $1,000 you get back.

### Example: why yield and coupon are different
*Example numbers.* A bond has 1 year left. It will pay a $40 coupon and then
$1,000, so $1,040 in total.
- If you pay **$1,000** for it: you get $1,040 back. Gain = $40.
  $40 ÷ $1,000 = **4.0% yield**. (Same as the coupon.)
- If you pay **$990** for it: you still get $1,040 back. Gain = $50.
  $50 ÷ $990 = **5.05% yield**. (Higher than the coupon, because you paid less.)
- If you pay **$1,010**: gain = $30. $30 ÷ $1,010 = **2.97% yield**.

**Lower price → higher yield. Higher price → lower yield.** Same bond, same
payments, different price.

## Why this matters for Laura

If you buy a Treasury and **hold it until it matures**, you know *today* exactly
how many dollars you will get and *when*. Price changes along the way don't
change that. That is why bonds are the tool for a payment Laura *must* make on a
known date.

## Words to know
- **Face value** — the amount paid back at the end (usually $1,000).
- **Coupon** — the fixed interest the bond pays each year, as a % of face value.
- **Maturity** — the date the face value is paid back.
- **Yield** — the yearly return you lock in by buying at today's price and
  holding to maturity.

## Say it in one sentence
> A bond is a loan with a fixed payback date, and its yield is the yearly return
> you lock in by buying it at today's price and holding it to the end.
