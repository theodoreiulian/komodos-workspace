## One role, and it is an administrative one

Decided 23 Sep 2026. We do **not** carry standing subject-matter roles. There is
no "the bond person" or "the modelling person". Everyone works on everything.

| Role | Who | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Team Leader** | *(name)* | Sole point of contact with Wharton; submits every deliverable via SurveyMonkey Apply; owns the roster and school documentation |

⚠️ The Team Leader must be **≥16 on 28 Sep 2026**, must opt in to programme
communications, and changing leader after that date needs Wharton's approval.
This is a rules requirement, not a division of labour.

### Why we chose this

Four people is small. Siloing the case into five specialisms would mean each of
us understands a quarter of our own strategy — and at semifinals **all members
present**, on a case where any judge can ask any of us why the reserve is
discounted at the rate it is. Polyvalence is the honest answer to that exposure.
It also protects us against the real risk of a four-person team: if one person
owns a domain and gets ill in late October, that domain stops.

### What we give up, and how we cover it

Wharton's FAQ says teams "should assign different roles to members", and
criterion 4 rewards demonstrated teamwork. Standing roles are the easy way to
*evidence* division of labour. Without them, the evidence has to come from
somewhere else — so it comes from the task log below. **If work is not written
down as assigned to a named person, we have no record that we divided it.**

The second thing we give up is a default owner for the standing obligations
nobody is excited to do. Those are named explicitly in "Standing obligations"
below and rotate.

## How work actually gets assigned

Task by task, at the weekly meeting, to a named person, with a date.

1. Work is broken into **one-off tasks**, not territories. "Check which Treasury
   maturities WInS lists, by Thursday" — not "you own fixed income".
2. Every task is written into **`06-team/task-log.md`** the moment it is
   assigned: what, who, due when. One line.
3. When it is done, the line gets a date and a pointer to the output — a script,
   a file, a decision-log entry.
4. Nobody works the same area twice running where we can avoid it. If one person
   built the ladder model, someone else stress-tests it.
5. **Every investment decision remains a team decision.** The FAQ is explicit
   that trades "should be a team decision". Individual tasks produce inputs;
   they never produce positions.

### Standing obligations (rotating, assigned weekly like anything else)

These have no permanent owner, so they must be assigned every week or they will
be missed:

- **Decision log** — write up the week's decisions, including reversals
- **Reproducibility check** — every number heading for a deliverable traces to a
  script in `03-modeling/` or a cited source
- **Rules check** — re-read the constraints that bite (word counts, formatting,
  trade limits) against whatever we are about to submit
- **Trading note review** — notes written *before* trades, and matching them

## Rhythm

| Cadence | What |
|---|---|
| **Weekly, fixed slot** | Strategy meeting. Review positions, decide trades, **assign the coming week's tasks and the standing obligations**, log decisions. |
| Before every trade | Write the Trading Note *first*. Two people minimum agree. |

All deadlines are **5:00 p.m. ET = 11:00 p.m. in France**. No extensions are
granted, ever. Target submission 24 hours early.

## Working principles

1. **Everyone can explain the whole case.** Not just the part they last worked
   on. This is the point of having no roles; if it stops being true, the model
   has failed and we should say so in the decision log.
2. **Trading Note before trade.** It is the evidence of reasoning, and Wharton
   verifies notes against executed trades.
3. **Disagreement gets logged.** A documented internal debate is exactly the
   "response to challenges" material criterion 4 asks for.
4. **Plain language.** If we cannot explain a position to someone outside the
   team in one sentence, we do not understand it yet.
5. **Few trades.** 200 is a ceiling, not a target. $25 a trade, and discipline is
   itself evidence.
6. **Everyone writes.** The Final Report must sound like a team, and all members
   must present at semifinals/finale if we get there.
