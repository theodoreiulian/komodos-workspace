# Strategy decision — protect operations, back promises, grow uncommitted money

**Owner:** Codex, 4 November 2026. **Status:** strategy framework adopted;
implementation and funding audit remain open. This is not an order list or a
submission-ready IPS. It supersedes the literal 30 September claim that all ten
payments could be fully locked with the first contribution on day one.

## Client analysis: what actually changes our decisions

The original case gives Laura an entrepreneurial attitude, experience in both
technology and creative work, and a community-oriented residency mission. It
also gives her specific obligations. Those obligations, rather than a guessed
personality score, determine how much investment risk each dollar can bear.

| Case fact | Investment implication | Overreach to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| $300,000 arrives in January 2027 and $150,000 in January 2028 | Buy only with money already received; the second contribution first completes operating protection | Treating $450,000 as available in 2027, or borrowing the gap |
| Ten fixed nominal $50,000 payments, January 2033–2042 | Match dollars and payment dates with Treasury payments and cash | Requiring equity growth to cover a fixed bill, or assuming fund payouts are contractual |
| Living expenses and short-term needs are funded outside this portfolio | Do not invent an emergency living-expense withdrawal | Diverting the stipulated contribution because of a speculative personal-income forecast |
| A building contribution is discretionary, with no fixed target | Take growth risk on surplus only and let the gift respond to outcomes | Promising a large building gift before the operating commitment is covered |
| A range must be communicated to sponsors in 2031 | Risk must fall when money becomes a public commitment, before the 2033 spending date | Treating the entire period through 2033 as equally long-term |
| Overpromising could damage sponsor confidence | Back the lower end before announcing it; show what remains uncertain | A model percentile presented as a guarantee |
| She wants flexibility as the project develops | Leave part of the surplus unpromised and restrict extra giving | Spending every remaining dollar or claiming retained money cannot lose value |
| Taiwan residency; obligations stated in US dollars | Protect the stated dollar payments; discuss purchasing power separately | Buying Taiwan stocks or euro bonds merely for geographic storytelling |
| Creative/technology background; thoughtful risk-taking | Broad global ownership, moderate risk on surplus, clear explanations | A presumed ethical technology exclusion or an unmeasured AI-income hedge |

Her mission shapes which outcome we protect: the artists' annual operating
support and the trust needed to attract sponsors. It does not require the
portfolio to resemble the arts industry. The research review supports possible
creative-industry disruption but cannot measure Laura's own earnings exposure
or an offsetting stock position. We reject the old “Laura is already short AI”
investment thesis. We do not assume missing income in the main case.

## What the competition rewards

The archived guide explicitly says simulator ranking, returns, outperformance
and trade count are not the scorecard. The recorded five criteria require a
clear strategy, client understanding, reasonable analysis, authentic learning,
and credible presentation. There are no published weights in the repository;
treating every criterion seriously is our working choice, not an official
equal-weight formula.

Our answer to each criterion:

1. **Strategy:** one hierarchy of promises, with specific changes in 2028,
   2031 and 2033 and a payment-by-payment plan afterward.
2. **Client:** protect the residency's operations and Laura's sponsor credibility;
   preserve room for an entrepreneur to adapt rather than use generic risk labels.
3. **Analysis:** show feasible contribution timing, dated cash flows, fund payout
   sensitivities, all-safe and higher-growth alternatives, and conditional ranges.
4. **Experience:** show actual research reversals and students' real understanding;
   do not invent student participation in AI work. The discovered financing error
   is evidence of a correction, not a victory until the replacement is tested.
5. **Presentation:** explain that a dollar becomes less free to take risk once
   Laura promises it. Show cash-flow evidence in the Final Report; keep the IPS
   within its no-graphics/no-citations requirements.

We cannot establish that no competing team will use a ladder or a similar
framework. Distinctiveness is a defensible application of the sponsor timeline,
not a claim to have invented liability matching or an estimate of others' work.

## The policy we adopt

### 2027–2028: protect operations before investing for the building

Start the operating-payment allocation in 2027 with no more than the available
$300,000. Use the 2028 contribution to complete and repair protection first;
only the verified remainder becomes Gift Portfolio capital. A bond is a loan
with specified interest and repayment dates. A ladder is several such holdings
whose payments arrive before the bills they are intended to fund.

Prefer nominal US Treasury cash flows and cash. Where the approved simulator
universe requires Treasury funds that close in a particular year, audit their
holdings, distributions, expenses and termination mechanics; stress their
proceeds rather than count them as fixed repayment promises. Do not assume
coupons earn a future reinvestment rate in the minimum-coverage schedule.

Define high funding certainty as sufficient available cash at **every** required
date under the stated conservative payment, cost and fund-payout assumptions,
without relying on stock returns or outside rescue. Report the remaining issuer,
fund and operational risks; separately report any model-based probabilities.
No numerical success probability or sufficient cushion is certified today.

If protecting the payments costs more, reduce growth capital first. If the
actual cash-feasible schedule cannot support the commitment, state that problem
instead of financing it implicitly or assuming exceptional stock returns.

### Until 2031: moderate global growth on verified surplus

Adopt a **40% global-stock / 60% short US Treasury-or-cash target within the
Gift Portfolio**, not within the whole account. Use broad low-cost holdings;
no dedicated AI, arts, Taiwan or technology bet. Actual instruments must be
eligible and verified in WInS. The short holding must be checked for price risk;
it is not automatically cash.

Review annually and rebalance after the operating coverage check. A deficit
is repaired before adding growth risk. This target is a policy judgment, not a
personal risk preference given by Laura or a statistically optimal percentage.

### 2031: back the lower end before it is communicated

First recheck operating protection and deduct needed repairs and costs. From
the remaining Gift balance, move **75% into cash**, quoting only its net funded
amount as the lower-end facility budget. Assume zero interest on this cash.
Leave **25% unpromised**, managed under the same 40/60 target. The amount left
unpromised is the flexibility reference for spending decisions; it is not a
separate contingency/endowment fund recommendation.

These are deliberate choices: 75% backs a substantial contribution while 25%
remains uncommitted. They are not inferred from a required confidence level.
The alternatives 50%, 90% and 100% were modeled: a larger lock leaves less
uncommitted capital; a smaller one backs a smaller sponsor contribution.

We do not assume an unverified two-year Treasury ending in 2033 exists in the
approved universe. Cash avoids that assumption and the need to forecast a
future rollover rate. Its cost is forgone interest and purchasing power.

The range is conditional: a cash-backed component plus market-dependent upside,
with the chance of the **actual donation** lying between both bounds evaluated
under explicit assumptions. The Final Report must show modeled dollar ranges
and confidence now, using projected 2031 conditions, and the rule for updating
them when the actual 2031 balance is observed. We do not defer all calculations
until real 2031 or call the simulated coverage real-world certainty.

### 2033 and afterward: honor the hierarchy and retain discretion

Formally set aside the operating reserve, then make the first operating payment,
then determine the building gift. The backed lower-end budget is the intended
facility commitment while operating assumptions remain satisfied.

Extra giving is **capped at gains above the capital left unpromised in 2031**,
and is still subject to a fresh flexibility and operating-funding check. Giving
that extra amount is not compulsory. This avoids consuming all residual capital
simply because markets did well. If unpromised investments lose value, less
flexibility remains; we do not guarantee preservation of that capital.

After the sponsor announcement and before the building gift is paid, operating deficits take priority.
Use unpromised capital first; if insufficient, reduce the facility budget and
disclose the changed range to sponsors. The same dollar cannot back both bills
and a gift. A conditional floor is not an irrevocable guarantee. After the gift
is paid, it cannot be reclaimed: remaining coverage checks and retained capital
are the available defenses. Show any unresolved shortfall honestly rather than
assuming a gift clawback or outside rescue.

For 2034–2042, keep upcoming payments in cash and hold later matched holdings
to receive their payments. Recheck remaining coverage annually, allow the reserve
to shrink as bills are paid, and do not replenish risky exposure in the reserve
to chase a return target.

## Why these choices survived the alternatives

The new model begins with **100 units of genuinely available Gift capital in
January 2028**, excluding operating money. Inputs are assumptions: base annual
stock mean 6.5%, short-asset return 3%, ordinary stock log-volatility 14%, a
separate annual crash regime, 0.15% annual drag and 0.10% execution costs.
The returns are not current quotes or calibrated forecasts. Pre-2031 growth
runs for three years; the cash backing then lasts two years at zero interest.

With a 75% cash lock, the base-model maximum-donation ceilings in 2033 are:

| Gift stock weight | Lower fifth-percentile ceiling | Middle ceiling | Upper 95th-percentile ceiling |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0% | 83.0 | 83.0 | 83.0 |
| **40% — adopted** | **71.2** | **87.1** | **105.1** |
| 60% | 65.6 | 89.2 | 117.4 |
| 100% | 54.1 | 92.9 | 144.6 |

These are **simulated ceilings per 100 starting units**, not final gifts or
dollar forecasts. The lower fifth percentile is the point below which 5% of
simulated ceilings fall; worse outcomes remain possible. Actual discretionary
giving can be below the ceiling.

Moving from 40% to 60% stocks raises the middle ceiling by 89.2 − 87.1 = 2.1
units, while lowering the downside ceiling by 71.2 − 65.6 = 5.6 units. We choose
the steadier sponsor capacity rather than that extra middle-case growth. We
reject all stocks because a bad market before 2031 reduces what Laura can
credibly announce; a later cash lock cannot undo the earlier loss.

**All-safe is the strongest objection.** It wins downside in this model and is
the better policy if risk earns no reward. We retain moderate stock ownership
because the building gift is discretionary and Laura seeks growth as well as
protection. This is an honest tradeoff, not a claim that stocks create safety.
Sensitivity runs include pessimistic returns, no equity premium, optimistic
returns, fees, pre-announcement crashes and post-announcement crashes.

## Corrections and remaining evidence

The legacy model put the full-scale ladder at $319,892 in January 2027:
$319,892 − $300,000 = **$19,892 unavailable cash**. Its S1 simulation carried
negative cash until the second contribution and set operating success to true
by construction. The fund payout approximation also did not validate the 2%
cushion. We withdraw the “all ten locked on day one” and validated “100% funded”
claims. The underlying operating-first idea survives; its implementation must
be rebuilt and tested. Original executed Trading Notes remain immutable.

Still required before exact orders or a claim of completed funding protection:
the cash-feasible staged holding schedule, conservative fund payout checks,
current holdings/cash/quotes, approved instrument availability, and an explicit
mapping between the simulator snapshot and the real two-contribution plan.
The normalized Gift model does not certify any of these.

Independent red-team and rules reviews support the framework with the operating
priority and discretionary-ceiling corrections incorporated above. The judge
review finds the idea memorable but calls operating proof and genuine student
experience the weak points. Reviewer scores are judgments, not probabilities
of selection.

**Revisit before 6 November** if the staged audit fails, realistic instrument
costs materially reduce available surplus, or stronger joint operating/Gift
tests reverse the tradeoff. After the freeze, apply the stated review, top-up,
range-update and spending rules; do not redesign the strategy after seeing
outcomes.
