# KB5 — Glossary

Terms we should be able to use correctly and, more importantly, **explain in one
plain sentence**. Judge Melissa Ko Hahn told teams to "fight against" complex
terminology — so for each term the second column is what we would actually say to
Laura.

| Term | In plain words |
|---|---|
| Annuity-due | A series of equal payments where the first one is made immediately. Laura's ten payments are an annuity-due. |
| Basis point (bp) | One hundredth of a percentage point. |
| Beta | How much an investment tends to move when the whole market moves. |
| Block bootstrap | Simulating the future by replaying real historical stretches of market history, so crashes stay realistic. |
| CAPE (Shiller P/E) | Price divided by ten years of inflation-adjusted earnings. A long-horizon valuation gauge. |
| Cash-flow matching | Buying bonds that mature exactly when you need the money, so you never have to sell anything. |
| Convexity | How a bond's sensitivity to interest rates itself changes as rates move. |
| Correlation | Whether two investments tend to move together. Low correlation is what makes diversification work. |
| CVaR (expected shortfall) | If things go badly, how bad is the average bad case. |
| Defeasance | Setting money aside in a way that permanently discharges a future obligation. |
| Drawdown | How far an investment has fallen from its highest point. |
| Duration | Roughly, how many years' worth of interest-rate sensitivity a bond has. |
| Efficient frontier | The set of portfolios giving the most expected return for each level of risk. |
| Equity risk premium | The extra return investors expect for owning stocks instead of government bonds. |
| Expense ratio | The annual fee a fund charges, as a percentage. |
| Funded ratio | What you have, divided by what you owe. |
| Glidepath | A pre-agreed plan for gradually shifting from growth investments to safe ones as a deadline approaches. |
| Human capital | The value of someone's future earnings — usually their biggest asset. |
| Immunisation | Arranging bonds so that interest-rate moves don't change your ability to pay a future bill. |
| Ladder | Owning bonds that mature in consecutive years, one for each payment. |
| Liability | A payment you are committed to making on a known date. |
| Liquidity | How quickly you can turn something into cash without moving its price. |
| Mean–variance optimisation | Maths that picks portfolio weights to balance expected return against volatility. |
| Monte Carlo simulation | Running thousands of possible futures to see the range of outcomes. |
| Nominal vs real | Nominal is the plain dollar amount; real adjusts for inflation. |
| Present value (PV) | What a future payment is worth today. |
| Rebalancing | Selling what has grown and buying what has lagged, to return to target weights. |
| Reinvestment risk | The risk that when money comes back to you, you can only reinvest it at a lower rate. |
| Sequence-of-returns risk | When you are paying money in or out, the *order* of good and bad years changes your outcome. |
| Sharpe ratio | Return above cash, per unit of volatility. |
| Sortino ratio | Like Sharpe, but only penalises downside moves. |
| STRIPS | Treasury bonds split so they make a single payment at maturity and nothing before. |
| Surplus | Assets minus liabilities. The part that is genuinely yours to spend. |
| TIPS | US government bonds whose payments rise with inflation. |
| Tracking error | How far a portfolio drifts from its benchmark. |
| VaR | The loss you would not expect to exceed, most of the time. |
| Volatility (σ) | How much something bounces around. Not the same thing as risk. |
| Yield curve | Interest rates plotted against how long you lend for. |
