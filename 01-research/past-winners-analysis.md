## Seven patterns worth acting on

### 1. Persistence is a real strategy
DMV's Finest (TJHSST) placed **3rd in 2024 after winning in 2023**, and had
*lost* the year before their win. Team member Om Gole: *"it's a perfect story…
we didn't win last year, we still had the resilience to come back."* Bergen
County Academies placed 2nd in 2022 and 1st in 2024. Stuyvesant's **FigCapital
reached the 2025 top ten and then won in 2026.**

→ Strong programmes iterate. We are year one. Our realistic aim is a
**semifinal place** (top 50 of ~2,300, roughly 1 in 45), and to build a repo that
next year's Komodos inherit rather than rebuild. That framing also improves this
year's work, because it makes documentation a first-class output.

### 2. Quantitative sophistication is noticed — but only as a means
Across finale write-ups, judges name specific tools: **efficient-frontier
analysis and portfolio theory** (Vikas Keswani, 2024), **algorithms and machine
learning** described as "next level" (Eric Balchunas, 2024), and reporting from
2025 lists **sentiment analysis, SWOT, PESTEL, customised algorithms, AI
innovations, portfolio optimisation and "performance readiness indices."**

→ So quant work is a differentiator at the top. But note *what* judges praise: the
2025 finale judge's summary of the deciding factor was **"clear thoughtfulness
and reasoning behind all the decisions"** — not the machinery. Our rule: every
model must terminate in a decision we can state in one sentence without the model.

### 3. Complexity for its own sake is punished
2025 semifinal judge Melissa Ko Hahn (Wharton MBA, Covepoint Capital) told teams
she would encourage them to **"fight against"** overly complex terminology,
noting that "the best solutions" are often straightforward.

→ This is the most actionable single piece of judge feedback on record. It also
directly contradicts the instinct of a technically strong team. **Sophisticated
analysis, plain words.** Build the complex model; publish the simple sentence.

### 4. The human element is the tiebreaker at the top
2025 client Ladi Ayoola, after watching the finals: *"It was very evident that
you put so much work into the research, analysis and presentations. But beyond
that was the human part."* And: *"Success in investing and in life isn't just
about the numbers; it's about understanding people."* 2024 judge Zoe McCormick:
*"When analyzing someone's investments, take a look at this person holistically."*

→ Three separate years, same message. At the top of the distribution everyone can
do the finance. The winner is the team that clearly understood a person.
For us: Laura's creative career is not colour — it is **an input to the asset
allocation**. See Angle A in `differentiation-angles.md`.

### 5. Winning teams win on values-alignment with a specific client
The 2025–26 case (Connor Barwin, Make The World Better) made "balancing financial
returns with community impact" central, and the 2023 finale coverage notes teams
treating **ESG considerations as critical pillars** of their strategies. Teams
that won did not bolt on a values section; they made the client's *actual
mission* a structural feature of the portfolio.

→ Laura's mission is storytelling, identity, belonging, and a physical community
space for creators in Taiwan. The honest version of values-alignment here is not
a generic ESG screen — it is asking what it means to invest the savings of a
creator whose industry is being restructured by the very technology driving the
market she is invested in. That tension is the case's gift to us.

### 6. Presentation craft matters and is cheap to get right
Finale coverage repeatedly cites "professional slide decks," "international
market analysis," "carefully crafted portfolios." The competition's own summary
of how to win: *"learn all you can, work together as a team, create a compelling
and creative investment strategy… articulate your strategy and overall competition
experience authentically and in such a way that you captivate the judges."*

→ "Captivate" is doing work in that sentence. So is "authentically."

### 7. Geography is not a barrier — and may be an asset
Finalists in recent years: Brazil, the UK (Westminster, The Perse), India
(Neerja Modi, Amity Noida), Czechia, Canada. **Lycée Français de Chicago placed
2nd in 2025 as "Finance from France."** Non-US teams reach the top ten regularly.

→ We should not hide being a French, international-section team. A genuinely
European vantage point — on ECB vs Fed divergence, on euro-area sovereigns
(which are *in* our permitted bond universe: DE, FR, IT, NL), on currency — is
both authentic voice and real analytical content that most US teams cannot write.

## ⚠️ Important caveat: this year's competition is structurally different

Do not over-fit to past winners. The 2026–27 cycle has been **substantially
redesigned**:

| | Previous cycles | 2026–27 |
|---|---|---|
| Virtual capital | $100k, then $500k (2025–26) | **$300,000** |
| Trading window | ~Oct–Feb | **6 weeks** (28 Sep – 6 Nov) |
| Mid-cycle deliverable | Mid-term report | **Trading Notes Analysis** (new) |
| Strategy document | Inside the final report | **Standalone IPS, frozen 6 Nov** (new) |
| Final report deadline | 12 Dec | **4 Dec** |
| Client goal type | General wealth/impact goals | **A hard, dated, ten-year liability** |

The new shape says a lot about intent. A standalone, word-limited, graphics-free
IPS that locks before the Final Report is a device for testing **strategic
discipline**: can you commit to a framework in advance and then live with it?
And a client goal expressed as a defined ten-year payment stream is, in
institutional terms, a **pension liability**. Wharton has quietly turned a
wealth-accumulation case into a **liability-driven investing** case.

Teams that reuse last year's "growth portfolio + ESG + Monte Carlo" template will
be answering the previous question. That is our opening.
