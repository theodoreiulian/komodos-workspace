# The Evaluation Criteria, decoded

The five criteria below are quoted **verbatim** from the competition website.
No weightings are published. The FAQ says: *"we are looking for teams that score
exceptionally well on all five components of the criteria."* Treat them as
equally weighted and as a **conjunctive** test — being brilliant at one and
mediocre at another does not get us to the semifinals.

Under each, what it actually demands of us and how we will earn it.

---

## 1. Investment Strategy
> "Presents a clear and creative investment thesis; demonstrates disciplined
> planning across Laura's changing time horizons and cash-flow needs; uses
> appropriate diversification; and maintains consistency with the team's IPS."

**Four things are being tested:** clarity, creativity, discipline across *changing*
horizons, and internal consistency.

- "Clear and creative **thesis**" — singular. We need one idea, expressible in a
  sentence, that a judge repeats back to us. Not a list of good practices.
- "Across Laura's **changing** time horizons" — the accumulation phase (2027–32),
  the pivot year (2033) and the decumulation phase (2033–42) are three different
  investment problems. A strategy that only describes one of them fails here.
- "Consistency with the team's IPS" — this is the trap. The IPS is frozen on
  6 Nov and the Final Report is judged partly on whether it obeys it. So the IPS
  must be written to be *survivable*: state a decision **framework** and
  **rules**, not point forecasts we will want to walk back in December.

## 2. Client Knowledge and Objectives
> "Tailors the strategy to Laura's circumstances, priorities, risk considerations,
> and residency goals; demonstrates a thoughtful understanding of the client; and
> presents recommendations that can earn her confidence."

- "**Earn her confidence**" is a communication test, not an analysis test. Would
  Laura — a smart non-specialist — read our recommendation and feel safe?
- The clients have said this out loud. 2025 client Ladi Ayoola: *"Success in
  investing and in life isn't just about the numbers; it's about understanding
  people."* And after judging: *"beyond that was the human part."*
- Laura's *circumstances* include things the case does not spell out but that
  follow from it: lumpy creative income, an IP-based business, a project sited in
  Taiwan, a stated willingness to take "thoughtful risks," and an explicit worry
  about over-promising and losing credibility. Each is a hook for tailoring.

## 3. Portfolio Analysis
> "Demonstrates understanding and effective use of investment concepts and tools;
> integrates quantitative and qualitative analysis; and uses reasonable
> assumptions and projections to evaluate funding reliability, the facility
> contribution, and financial flexibility under varying market outcomes."

- This is where rigour is rewarded: **"under varying market outcomes"** is an
  instruction to model distributions, not a single base case.
- "**Integrates** quantitative and qualitative" — numbers alone lose. So do
  narratives alone. Each figure needs a sentence of meaning.
- "**Reasonable** assumptions" — defensible and stated, not optimistic. Judges
  have rewarded efficient-frontier work, portfolio theory, algorithms and machine
  learning in past years, but only when it explained a decision.

## 4. Articulation of Competition Experience
> "Demonstrates teamwork, communication, and learning; clearly explains the team's
> research and decision-making process; and reflects meaningfully on the team's
> growth and response to challenges."

- The most-neglected criterion, and the cheapest to win. It rewards an honest
  account of **how we worked and what we got wrong** — which we can only write if
  we keep a decision log from day one. Start now.
- "Response to challenges" wants a real challenge with a real resolution. A
  strategy we revised after evidence contradicted it is *better* material than a
  strategy that never wobbled.

## 5. Creativity and Presentation
> "Presents a compelling, well-organized narrative in an authentic team voice;
> uses data effectively to support conclusions; demonstrates original thinking and
> meaningful reflection; and communicates Laura's potential facility contribution
> and investment uncertainty clearly and credibly to prospective co-sponsors."

- "**Authentic team voice**" — four French-based students at an international
  school in Saint-Germain-en-Laye, several of us bilingual, working across a
  six-hour time difference from the market we are trading. That is a voice.
  Committee-speak erases it; so does over-polished AI prose.
- The co-sponsor communication is called out *inside* this criterion. It is a
  **persuasive writing task aimed at a non-financial reader**, and it is graded.
  Treat it as a real fundraising document, not an appendix.

## The scoring model we will hold in our heads

Almost every submitted report will be *competent*: a diversified portfolio, a
sensible glidepath, some Monte Carlo. Competence gets you into the 2,300. What
separates the 50 is a **thesis with an edge** — one idea that is specific to
*this* client, defensible in numbers, and explainable in a sentence — plus
**writing that sounds like people**. We optimise for those two things.
